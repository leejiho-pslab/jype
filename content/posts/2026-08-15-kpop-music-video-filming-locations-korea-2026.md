## Ever Wondered Where Your Favorite K-Pop Music Videos Were Actually Filmed?

I'll be honest: when I first moved to Seoul, I spent an embarrassing amount of time trying to recreate that one Stray Kids scene from "특 (S-Class)"—you know, the one with the neon street at sunset. Turns out it was filmed just 20 minutes from my apartment, and I'd walked past it a hundred times without realizing it. That's when I realized how many international K-pop fans visit Korea every year hoping to do exactly what I accidentally stumbled into: **stand in the same spot where their favorite idols shot their most iconic music videos**.

The good news? **You don't need insider knowledge or a tour guide to find these locations**. The even better news is that many K-pop filming sites in Korea are publicly accessible, beautifully photogenic, and genuinely worth the trip—whether you're a hardcore ARMY, ENGENE, or just a casual K-culture traveler looking for an extra dimension to your Korea trip. In this guide, I'm sharing the real K-pop music video locations you can actually visit right now in 2026, complete with directions, what to expect, and tips for getting the best shots.

## IVE's Iconic Urban Filming Spots: Where "I AM" and "I" Were Shot

IVE's music videos are known for their sleek, modern aesthetic, and **most of their 2024–2026 filming happens in Seoul's trendier districts**. The group's "I AM" music video—one of their most visually striking releases—was partially filmed at **Seokchon-dong (석촌동), the historic neighborhood near Gyeongbokgung Palace**. The area has traditional Korean hanok houses mixed with contemporary cafés, making it perfect for that contrast IVE loves.

Getting there is straightforward: take Seoul Metro Line 3 to Gyeongbokgung Palace Station (Exit 5), then walk about 10 minutes downhill into the neighborhood. You'll pass antique shops and photography studios—it's genuinely beautiful even without the K-pop connection.

Another IVE video location that surprised many fans was **Yangjae Citizens' Forest (양재시민의숲)**, a sprawling park in southern Seoul. It features in several of their recent MV concepts and is free to visit. The forest has walking trails, open meadows, and seasonal flowers, making it ideal for early morning photo sessions if you're trying to recreate those ethereal, sun-flare shots. As of August 2026, there's no entrance fee, though parking costs approximately ₩3,000 per hour if you're driving.

Pro tip: **fansite photographers often gather at these locations on weekends**, so if you're serious about nailing the exact angle, morning visits (before 8 a.m.) are usually quieter. Check Korean K-pop forums or Twitter (X) K-pop accounts a day or two before you visit to see if other fans have recently posted location updates.

## Stray Kids' Cinematic Highway and Urban Streets: The "특" (S-Class) Route

Stray Kids has made a name for themselves with high-budget, cinematic music videos that often feature dramatic street shots and moody urban aesthetics. Their "특 (S-Class)" music video is one of the most recognizable in K-pop right now, and **the main filming location is along the Gangseo Digital Complex overpass area (강서디지털단지역)**, just west of central Seoul.

The overpass itself isn't difficult to find once you know where to look—take Metro Line 9 to Gangseo Digital Complex Station—but I'll be upfront: **it's not the most scenic spot on its own**. What makes it special is the neon lighting setup Stray Kids' production team installed, plus the angle they filmed from. If you visit during evening hours, the area does have some atmospheric urban lighting, though it won't be identical to the MV's heavily color-corrected shots.

What I found more rewarding was visiting **Ttukseom Hangang Park (뚝섬 한강공원)**, another location featured in their 2024–2025 releases. The park offers gorgeous riverside views and is popular with other K-pop groups too (Seventeen has filmed here multiple times). **The park is completely free to enter and open sunrise to sunset**, and it's one of the few spots where you can get genuinely Instagram-worthy shots that feel close to the original MV aesthetic.

**Getting there:** Metro Line 2 or 7 to Ttukseom Station. Arrive around 5–6 p.m. for the best lighting if you're chasing that sunset-MV vibe.

## LE SSERAFIM, NewJeans & Other Groups: Trending Coastal and Mall Locations

If you're into **LE SSERAFIM** or **NewJeans**, you'll notice their recent music videos often feature coastal highways, train stations, and indoor mall settings—locations that feel very "modern Korea tourist experience." NewJeans' "Attention" was partially filmed at **Busan's Songdo Skywalk (부산 송도 스카이워크)**, a glass-floored walkway suspended over the sea that's become hugely popular with international tourists.

The Skywalk costs approximately **₩6,000 per person** (as of August 2026) and takes about 30–40 minutes to walk in full. The views are stunning, and yes, it really does look as dramatic as it does in K-pop videos. **Book your ticket online via Naver or Kakao to skip queues**, especially during peak summer season (July–August).

For **LE SSERAFIM fans**, their "Good Bones" music video featured filming at **Incheon's New City area**, including the Incheon Bridge backdrop. While the bridge itself is off-limits to pedestrians, you can get close-up shots from the surrounding roads and parks. **The Incheon Songdo Central Park** (인천 송도 센트럴 파크) is a great complementary visit—it has that same modern, polished aesthetic many K-pop groups are favoring in 2026.

## How to Plan Your Own K-Pop Location Tour

If you're serious about visiting multiple locations, here's a realistic itinerary:

1. **Pick 3–4 groups you follow** and search their filmography on Korean wiki sites (Naver or Google, then translate—it's easier than it sounds).
2. **Check if a fandom has already mapped the locations**. Sites like "kpop.map" and Twitter threads from location scouts are goldmines.
3. **Screenshot the exact GPS coordinates** from Google Maps before you go, especially if the spot doesn't have a official landmark.
4. **Visit during off-peak hours** (weekday mornings or late afternoons) to avoid crowds and get cleaner shots.
5. **Bring backup battery and water**, especially if you're island-hopping between multiple coastal locations.

**Important note:** Some filming locations are on private property or inside office buildings, so always check if a location is genuinely public before showing up. If a location is marked as "private residence" or "company office," respect the boundary—many fansite photographers have been asked to leave by security guards.

## Real Talk: What the Music Videos Don't Show You

I should be honest about one thing: **K-pop music videos use professional color grading, timing, and angles that make locations look dramatically better than they do in real life**. The sunset at Ttukseom Hangang Park will never be *exactly* as neon-orange as it looks in a Stray Kids MV because those videos use saturation boosts. The highway overpasses are genuinely urban and sometimes a bit grimy up close.

**But here's why that doesn't matter:** the act of standing where your favorite artists stood, getting the photo angle right, and connecting that physical place to the music you love creates a real memory that's worth way more than a perfect recreation. I've met K-pop fans from the US, Brazil, and Indonesia at these locations, and every single one said the same thing: "It's not exactly the same, but being here makes the music feel more real."

As of August 2026, many locations have added small signage indicating "this location was featured in [artist name] music video," so the tourism boards in Seoul and nearby cities have started recognizing the K-pop pilgrimage trend.

## Key Takeaways: Your K-Pop Location Checklist

- **IVE fans**: Start with Seokchon-dong and Yangjae Citizens' Forest; early morning visits yield the best photos.
- **Stray Kids fans**: Ttukseom Hangang Park is more rewarding than Gangseo Complex for Instagram shots.
- **NewJeans/LE SSERAFIM fans**: Coastal locations (Busan Skywalk, Incheon Songdo) require a day trip but are worth it for the scenery.
- **Pro tip**: Check fandom Twitter/X before your trip to see if anyone has recently posted location updates.
- **Always verify** locations are public and safe to access before visiting. Check official tourism sites like [Visit Korea](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr) for current accessibility info.
- **Budget tip**: Most core locations are free or under ₩10,000 total; don't pay for unofficial "K-pop tour packages" unless they include transportation.

Remember: **you don't need to be a superfan to enjoy these spots**—they're genuinely beautiful places that happen to be featured in music videos. Even if you're not deeply into K-pop, locations like Ttukseom Park and Seokchon-dong are worthwhile Seoul experiences on their own.

Planning a Korea trip? Add 2–3 of these to your itinerary. You won't regret it.
