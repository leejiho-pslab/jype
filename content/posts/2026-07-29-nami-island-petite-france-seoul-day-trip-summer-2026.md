If you've been sweating through Seoul in July and wondering where you can actually breathe again, the answer is a Seoul day trip to Nami Island — and if you time it right, a detour to the fairy-tale hilltop of Petite France on the same day. I did exactly that on a blazing Tuesday in late July 2026, and it turned out to be one of the best decisions of my Korean summer. Here's everything you need to know to pull it off smoothly.

---

## Why Nami Island and Petite France Are the Perfect Summer Escape from Seoul

These two destinations sit only about 10 km apart in Gyeonggi-do and Gangwon-do, making them natural day-trip partners that reward you with dramatically different vibes in a single outing.

**Nami Island (Naminara Republic)** is a small crescent-shaped island in the North Han River, famous for its cathedral-like rows of towering metasequoia and ginkgo trees. In summer those canopies turn a deep, saturated green that filters the sunlight into something almost cinematic — much cooler underfoot than the concrete jungle of central Seoul. The island became internationally iconic after the 2002 K-drama *Winter Sonata* was filmed here, and it still draws visitors from across Asia and beyond.

**Petite France** is a Cultural Village on a hillside overlooking Cheongpyeong Lake, styled after the villages of southern France. Think pastel-coloured buildings, *Le Petit Prince* installations everywhere, and a breezy hilltop position that catches the wind even on the hottest days. It also served as a filming location for the K-drama *My Love from the Star* and the variety show *Running Man*, so K-culture fans will recognise it immediately.

Together, they give you riverside shade, a breezy hilltop, and two very photogenic backdrops — the ideal formula for escaping Seoul's summer heat without sacrificing the 'gram.

## Getting There: Transport Options from Seoul (Step by Step)

Getting to both spots without a car is completely doable, and honestly not that complicated once you know the route.

**Option A — ITX-Cheongchun Train + Shuttle Bus (Recommended)**

1. Take Seoul Metro Line 2 to **Gapyeong Station** (환승 at Sangbong to the ITX-Cheongchun; total ~80 min from Hongdae).
2. At Gapyeong Station, board the **Gapyeong Tour Bus** (a single loop that stops at Nami Island Ferry Dock, Petite France, and the Garden of Morning Calm). The loop runs roughly every 30–60 minutes and costs about ₩5,000–₩8,000 per segment as of July 2026.
3. Cross to Nami Island by ferry (included in your entrance ticket — the ferry runs every 30 minutes).

**Option B — Direct Shuttle Bus from Seoul**

Several operators run direct round-trip shuttles from central Seoul (usually Hongdae or Myeongdong). Prices were around ₩10,000–₩15,000 per person as of July 2026. This is convenient but less flexible for combining both attractions.

**Option C — Rental Car / Kakao T**

If there are three or more of you, splitting a car rental or a Kakao T intercity taxi can actually beat the shuttle on both cost and flexibility.

| Route | Journey Time from Seoul | Approx. Cost (per person) | Flexibility |
|---|---|---|---|
| ITX Train + Tour Bus | ~90 min + bus hops | ₩10,000–₩18,000 | High |
| Direct shuttle bus | ~90 min | ₩10,000–₩15,000 | Low |
| Rental car / Kakao T | ~70 min | ₩15,000–₩30,000 (shared) | Very High |

Always confirm current schedules on the [Korea Tourism Organization (VisitKorea)](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr) website, as timetables shift seasonally.

One tip: buying a **Nami Island + Petite France combo ticket** through the Gapyeong Tour Bus operator can save you a small amount and simplifies logistics. Ask at Gapyeong Station's tourist booth when you arrive.

## My Recommended One-Day Itinerary for Both Spots

The secret to fitting both into a single day without feeling rushed is to hit Nami Island first thing in the morning before the crowds peak, then head to Petite France in the early afternoon.

**08:00** — Depart Seoul (Sangbong Station ITX-Cheongchun)
**09:20** — Arrive Gapyeong Station; grab a coffee and board the tour bus toward the ferry dock
**09:50** — Ferry to Nami Island; spend **2.5–3 hours** wandering the metasequoia path, the riverside walk, and the Ostrich Farm area. Eat lunch at one of the island's cafés (the *dakgalbi* rice bowl is solid).
**13:00** — Ferry back; board tour bus to Petite France (~15-min ride)
**13:20** — Arrive Petite France; spend **2–2.5 hours** exploring the alleys, the *Le Petit Prince* museum, and catching a performance
**15:30–16:00** — Return to Gapyeong Station by tour bus
**17:30** — Back in central Seoul

If you're visiting on a **weekday**, this schedule is relaxed. On summer weekends, add 30–45 minutes of buffer everywhere — both spots get genuinely crowded.

## Summer-Specific Tips: Beating the Heat and the Crowds

Summer in this region means **high 20s to mid-30s Celsius** (mid-80s to mid-90s°F) with high humidity, so going in without a plan is how you end up miserable.

- **Go early.** The metasequoia avenue on Nami Island is shaded, but by midday the open riverside areas are brutally sunny. I arrived just before 10 a.m. and had the famous tree avenue almost to myself for the first 30 minutes.
- **Pack light but smart.** Sunscreen, a small folding fan or pocket fan, and a reusable water bottle are non-negotiables. There are water refill points on Nami Island near the main plaza.
- **Petite France's hillside catches the breeze.** Even on still days in the valley, the hilltop position of Petite France tends to be noticeably cooler. Plan your outdoor lounging time there.
- **Avoid peak summer weekends if possible.** The ferry line at Nami Island on a Saturday in August can stretch to 40+ minutes. Weekday mornings are a completely different experience.
- **Download Kakao Maps** before you go — it gives real-time bus arrival info for the Gapyeong Tour Bus stops, which is genuinely useful for timing your hops between attractions.

According to [Korea.net](https://www.korea.net/), Nami Island receives over 3 million visitors annually, with summer being the second-busiest season after autumn. Going in with that context helps set expectations — and motivates you to plan around the crowds rather than into them.

## What to Eat and Buy: Local Flavours Around Gapyeong

The area around Gapyeong is known for a few regional specialities worth building into your day.

- **Dakgalbi (spicy stir-fried chicken):** Chuncheon, the city just north of Gapyeong, is the spiritual home of dakgalbi in Korea. Several restaurants near Gapyeong Station serve excellent versions — look for the places with handwritten menus and locals inside, not the ones facing tour buses.
- **Yuja (yuzu) products:** Local vendors sell yuja tea, yuja candy, and yuja jam, which make great gifts.
- **Chestnut snacks:** The hillside villages around Petite France sell roasted and candied chestnuts; odd in summer, but they're genuinely good.
- On Nami Island itself, there are a handful of cafés and a small bakery near the entry plaza. Prices are tourist-level (expect to pay ₩6,000–₩8,000 for a coffee), but the setting — literally sitting under a canopy of ancient trees — makes it worth it once.

This is genuinely one of the best Seoul day trips I've taken in summer, and the combination of Nami Island's green cathedral walks and Petite France's breezy hilltop pastels makes it feel like two countries in one afternoon — without ever leaving Korea.
